Schools Are Embracing AI. Some Parents Are Worried

USA Today・2026.10.23
Consent & PrivacyOver-reliance

A parent in South Carolina found an AI-generated photo in her first-grader's homework folder: her daughter at 25, working as a nurse, with long wavy hair that appeared nowhere in the child's own artwork. The girl had already made a picture of her grown-up self with paper and pencil; the AI replaced it with an algorithm's idea of what a successful adult woman looks like. The parent's Instagram post drew more than 600,000 likes. The story raises two questions: does AI crowd out children's imagination, and should teachers need parental consent before uploading students' photos to an AI platform? The consent form signed at the start of the year covered only publishing photos and work, with no mention of AI. One scholar notes that schools still lack clear rules on AI, so the burden should not fall on teachers alone, but identifiable student data should never go into AI models. The district halted similar assignments and announced AI literacy training for teachers. Which part of this assignment actually needed a computer?
