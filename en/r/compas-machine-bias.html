Machine Bias: Risk Assessments in Criminal Sentencing

ProPublica・2016.05.23
Bias & DiscriminationAlgorithmic Decisions

Many US courts use the COMPAS algorithm to score defendants' risk of reoffending. ProPublica examined data from one Florida county and found that the system systematically overestimated risk for Black defendants and underestimated it for white defendants. Among people who did not reoffend, about 45 percent of Black defendants but 23 percent of white defendants were labeled high risk; among those who did reoffend, about 48 percent of white defendants but 28 percent of Black defendants were labeled low risk. The story opens with two people: an 18-year-old Black woman who took a child's bicycle, rated high risk, and a white man with prior armed robbery convictions, rated low risk. Two years later, it was he who reoffended. The 137-question survey never asks about race, yet questions such as whether a parent was ever jailed are bound up with race and poverty. The company's founder conceded that removing them would make the tool less accurate. He also testified that the tool was not built for sentencing, but judges used it that way.
