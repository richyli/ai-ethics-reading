Air Canada Found Liable for Its Chatbot's Bad Advice

CBC News・2024.02
AI ErrorAccountability

On the day his grandmother died, a traveler asked the chatbot on Air Canada's website about bereavement fares. It told him he could buy a full-price ticket and claim the discount within 90 days. He did, and the airline then refused the refund, saying that was not its policy. At the British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal, Air Canada made a memorable argument: the chatbot was a separate legal entity responsible for its own actions. The tribunal rejected it outright. The chatbot is part of the company's website, customers have no reason to know which parts of a website are reliable, and the airline had failed to take reasonable care that its chatbot was accurate. An agent had even admitted the chatbot's wording was misleading and promised to flag it for an update, but fixing it afterward did not undo the harm. It was the first Canadian ruling on a chatbot giving wrong advice. When a company hands customer service to AI, can it hand off responsibility too?
