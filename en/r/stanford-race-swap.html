Stanford Uses AI to Race-Swap Students in Advertising

The Stanford Review・2026.09.21
Consent & PrivacyBias & DiscriminationDisclosure & Trust

Stanford's Residential & Dining Enterprises used generative AI to alter real student photos on campus advertising boards: one male student was replaced entirely by an AI-generated Black woman, and two other students in the shot were made visibly thinner. The student remembered the photo shoot but learned he had been swapped out only when a friend sent him a before-and-after comparison. He said he first found it funny, then felt silenced and erased. The same edit slimmed other students too, suggesting the institution treated swapping someone's race as routine retouching, no different from adjusting body shape. The issue is not whether the edit looked good, but that an institution altered the people in a documentary photo without their knowledge or consent. The campus publication that broke the story reads it as evidence of the university's long-standing racial preferences, but offers no further evidence for that claim. As you read, it is worth keeping two things apart: the event itself, and the interpretation placed on it.
