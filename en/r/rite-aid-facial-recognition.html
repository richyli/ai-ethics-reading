Rite Aid Banned from Using AI Facial Recognition

US Federal Trade Commission・2023.12.19
SurveillanceBias & DiscriminationAccountability

Between 2012 and 2020, US pharmacy chain Rite Aid used facial recognition in its stores to match shoppers against a list of suspected shoplifters. After investigating, the Federal Trade Commission banned the company from using facial recognition for surveillance for five years and ordered it to delete the images, along with any algorithms trained on them. The order extends to third parties that received the data, an important precedent for any organization that hands data to a vendor. The problem was not just that the system misidentified people, but that the company took almost no precautions: no accuracy testing before launch, a database built from blurry CCTV and employee phone photos, no training for staff on false matches, no notice to customers and no way to complain. False matches were more frequent in stores in mostly Black and Asian neighborhoods. Misidentified customers were followed, searched, ordered out and accused of theft in front of family and friends. The machine said "that's the person" and staff acted on it. Whose judgment was missing in between?
