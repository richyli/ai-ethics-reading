Amazon's AI Recruiting Tool Penalized Women

TechNews (Taiwan)・2018.10.17
Bias & DiscriminationAlgorithmic Decisions

Starting in 2014, Amazon built an AI tool that ranked job applicants for some 500 roles, trained on ten years of past résumés. The team later found that the model systematically scored women lower, and the project was disbanded in 2017. The flaw was not a coding bug but the training data: most past hires were men, so the model learned to treat the vocabulary of male applicants as a sign of a strong candidate. The article adds other cases, such as image searches for "doctor" returning mostly white men and photo software labeling Black people as gorillas, and an Indian wedding dress recognized as medieval chain mail because Western examples dominated the training data, to argue that AI absorbs existing social inequality and then amplifies it behind a seemingly objective score. A question to take away: when we hand hiring decisions to AI, how people were chosen in the past becomes how people will be chosen in the future. Who is responsible for checking that history?
