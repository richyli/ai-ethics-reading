How Indianapolis Newsrooms Are Using AI

Poynter・2026.05.20
Disclosure & Trust

Poynter's pilot public editor column in Indianapolis surveyed how local newsrooms use AI. Most are cautious: AI handles transcripts and captions, reporters check every quote themselves, and a human makes the final call on publishing. A few under-resourced outlets go further, using AI for graphics, editing and even an AI news anchor. Its editor sees AI as a way to compete with bigger institutions despite limited resources. The scholars, for their part, see uses that widen access, such as text-to-speech and translation, as good for the community. The journalism ethicists interviewed draw a red line: using AI to impersonate a real person, such as an AI reporter, is deceptive even when disclosed, because some audiences will still believe it is human. The article also gives readers three tests: does the outlet do its own original reporting, is its AI disclosure specific enough, and does it commit to keeping humans in the loop? As a reader, how would you judge how much of a news story was made by AI?
