McDonald's AI Pricing by Location

Business Next (Taiwan)・2026.09.30
Algorithmic DecisionsAccountability

A Reuters investigation found that McDonald's uses a machine learning platform to analyze daily sales at nearly 14,000 US restaurants and calculate an "optimal price" for every item at every location, with local customers' willingness to pay as one of the factors. Reuters found two company-owned stores in California about three kilometers apart where a Big Mac differed in price by about 20 percent, though it could not confirm that the gap came from the system. Headquarters calls the tool a recommendation and says franchisees set their own prices, yet it has written "constructive engagement" with the pricing tool into its operating standards and tracks who departs from suggested prices, leaving franchisees feeling pressured. The platform's own terms of service warn franchisees about antitrust law, which a former FTC commissioner read as an admission of potential problems. Headquarters earns a share of revenue while franchisees carry the costs, so optimal means different things to each. The question to ask is: optimal for whom? When the reason behind a price is a model no one can see into, who can customers and franchisees bargain with?
