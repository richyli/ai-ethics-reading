AI Is Making Workers Faster, and That Could Be a Problem

Newsweek・2026
Over-relianceAccountability

A workplace survey found that employees using AI save an average of 2.3 hours a day, yet half say they rely on it too much and 39 percent feel it has made them less smart; nearly half of Gen Z workers worry that AI is eroding their skills. The article draws a key distinction: AI can make people faster without making them better. The flip side of speed is a "review tax": more than half of employees must check colleagues' AI-generated work, and most find it slower to review than work done by people. Responsibility is murkier still: 83 percent fear being blamed for AI mistakes, and 43 percent have used AI output they suspected was wrong or made up. The article urges companies to separate honest mistakes made while following good practice from careless ones made by cutting corners, rather than driving AI use through fear. For anyone still learning, the warning is direct: the speed you gain by skipping practice may be paid for later with your own judgment.
