External Validation of a Widely Used Sepsis Prediction Model

JAMA Internal Medicine・2021.06.21
AI ErrorOver-relianceAccountability

Epic, a major electronic health record vendor, ships a sepsis prediction model built into the systems of hundreds of US hospitals, and the company reported a discrimination score (AUC) of 0.76 to 0.83. Michigan Medicine tested it independently on about 38,000 hospital stays and found just 0.63. In clinical terms, only one in eight alerts pointed to a real case of sepsis, while the model missed two-thirds of sepsis patients altogether, many of whom were treated in time thanks to clinicians' own judgment. The study is a reminder that wide adoption does not prove a system works, and that a vendor's own validation should not be the basis for purchasing decisions. The authors note that performance documents for proprietary models like this rarely go through peer review, which makes them hard to check from outside. When hospitals trust the vendor's numbers and front-line staff are buried in false alarms, who ends up bearing the cost?
