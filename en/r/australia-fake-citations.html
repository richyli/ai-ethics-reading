Senior Lawyer Apologizes for AI-Generated Fake Quotes in Murder Case

CBS News・2025.08.15
AI ErrorOver-reliance

In a youth murder trial before the Supreme Court of Victoria in Australia, the defense filed submissions containing a fabricated quote from a state parliament speech and court cases that did not exist. The judge's staff discovered the problem when they could not find the cited cases, and the verdict was delayed by 24 hours. Court documents did not even identify which AI tool was used. The defense team admitted they had checked the first few citations, found them correct and assumed the rest were too; the prosecution had not checked either. The judge criticized the episode and noted that the court had already ruled that AI output must be independently and thoroughly verified before use. The report links similar cases in the US and UK, where a senior judge warned that submitting fake AI citations could amount to perverting the course of justice. Incidents in three jurisdictions show this is no longer an isolated problem. The key lesson is the reasoning that if the first few are right, the rest must be too, one of the most common traps when people work with AI.
