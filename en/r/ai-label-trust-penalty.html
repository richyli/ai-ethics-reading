Why Labeling News as AI-Generated Lowers Trust in the Outlet

Yahoo! JAPAN News (Kazuhiro Taira)・2023.12.07
Disclosure & Trust

Japanese media researcher Kazuhiro Taira introduces a US experiment in which more than 1,400 participants read three AI-generated news stories. Those who saw a label saying the stories were produced entirely by AI rated the outlet as noticeably less trustworthy than those who saw no label, yet their ratings of the stories' accuracy and fairness barely changed. Readers penalized the news organization, not the content. More surprisingly, the people who trusted news most and knew most about how it is made penalized it the most. Those who knew less about news production actually trusted the outlet slightly more after seeing the label. This is the AI disclosure paradox: audiences ask for transparency, then punish the outlets that provide it. Nor do readers reject AI across the board: they accept it far more for routine news such as weather and stock prices than for politics. The article ends with a way forward: listing the news sources the AI drew on largely offsets the loss of trust. Disclosing AI is not just a matter of adding a label; readers need to understand how much the AI actually did.
