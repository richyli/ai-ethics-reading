Nantou Uses AI Cameras to Catch Illegal Straw Burning

United Daily News (Taiwan)・2026.06.24
SurveillanceAlgorithmic Decisions

To crack down on open-air straw burning, Nantou County in central Taiwan began using drones and elevated AI cameras in 2026. Image recognition automatically detects smoke and flames and alerts inspectors in real time. In the past, farmland was scattered, fires were brief, and violators chose early mornings or remote spots, so by the time inspectors arrived the person was gone; over two years, not a single case was cited. After the AI system arrived, inspectors cited nine of the ten cases they checked. The county's environmental chief stressed that the goal is not more fines but deterrence: people stop burning when they know someone is watching. The county plans to pair the cameras with night patrols and inter-agency work; the AI does not replace inspectors but sees the scene for them first. The story opens up the other side of AI surveillance. It does what human patrols cannot, yet its deterrent power comes from the feeling of always being watched. People change their behavior when they are seen, which is exactly why surveillance works and why it is unsettling. If the same technology were used elsewhere, how much would we accept being seen?
