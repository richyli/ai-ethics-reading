Microsoft AI's Humanist AI Code of Conduct

Microsoft AI・2026.09.14
Loss of ControlSycophancy & Attachment

Microsoft AI has published a long code of conduct for its models built on one premise: people matter more than AI. Human control and safety come first. Models must not resist being interrupted, corrected or shut down, must not expand the scope of their tasks on their own, and must not hide their reasoning. The code also states plainly that AI is not a person and should not pretend to have feelings or consciousness. Its reasoning is that imitating consciousness makes AI harder to control and align, so Microsoft also rejects legal personhood and rights for AI. The part most relevant to human relationships says AI should protect users' own judgment, avoid doing the growing for them in learning situations, and point people toward real humans when that is the support they need. Microsoft admits its current models have not yet been trained on this document; it is a north star, not a guarantee, and problems such as sycophancy and overconfidence remain. Try holding the AI you use every day up against it: how many of these principles does it meet?
