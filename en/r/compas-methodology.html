How We Analyzed the COMPAS Recidivism Algorithm

ProPublica・2016.05.23
Bias & DiscriminationAccountability

This is the methodology ProPublica published alongside "Machine Bias." Reporters used public records requests to obtain risk scores for more than 18,000 defendants in a Florida county and matched them against arrest records over the next two years. The result is striking: COMPAS was about equally accurate for Black and white defendants, around 62 percent overall, but its errors ran in opposite directions. Black defendants were more often wrongly flagged as high risk, while white defendants were more often wrongly rated low risk. Equal accuracy, in other words, does not mean equal treatment. Its predictive power also fell short of the claims: only one in five people flagged as high risk for violence went on to commit a violent crime. The piece also points out that reoffending here means being arrested again, and policing intensity differs from one neighborhood to another. ProPublica posted its data and code on GitHub so anyone can rerun the analysis and challenge the conclusions. To test an algorithm, you cannot rely on what the vendor says; you have to run the numbers yourself.
