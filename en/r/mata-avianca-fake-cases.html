Lawyer Used ChatGPT in Court and Cited Fake Cases

Forbes・2023.06.08
AI ErrorOver-relianceAccountability

In a US lawsuit against the airline Avianca, the plaintiff's lawyer used ChatGPT for legal research and cited at least six cases in a court filing. Opposing counsel could not find any of them, and the judge confirmed they were bogus decisions with bogus quotes. The lawyer admitted he thought ChatGPT was a search engine; he had even asked it whether the cases were real, and when it said yes, he believed it. He said he had tried it after reading that AI might make legal research obsolete. When asked to produce the full decisions, he went back to ChatGPT to generate them. The judge fined him $5,000, making clear that the absence of any intent to deceive did not excuse the failure to check. The gap between how AI is marketed and what it can actually do turned into a concrete mistake. It was the first time AI hallucination reached a US federal court, and many courts then began requiring lawyers to disclose their use of generative AI. The more confident the AI sounds, the easier it is to forget to verify.
